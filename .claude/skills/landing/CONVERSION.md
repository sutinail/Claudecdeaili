# Conversion Principles — Formulas & Patterns

---

## The Correct Section Order

This is the sales argument, not just a layout. Each section earns the right to ask for the next thing.

```
1. Hero          — "Here's the thing you need" + CTA
2. Social proof  — "Others already trust it" (earn trust before proving value)
3. Problem       — "You know this pain" (create recognition)
4. Solution      — "Here's the better way" (reframe the problem)
5. Features      — "Here's specifically what you get" (specificity builds belief)
6. Testimonials  — "Real people with real results" (proof at belief stage)
7. Pricing       — "Here's what it costs" (after value is established)
8. FAQ           — "Handle your last objections" (friction removal)
9. Final CTA     — "Still here? Here's how to start" (close again)
```

**Never lead with features.** Features before context = confused visitors.
**Never lead with pricing.** Pricing before value = sticker shock.
**Testimonials after features.** The testimonial validates the feature claim.

## Hero Section Formula

```
[Headline]    — What it is + who it's for (one sentence, ≤10 words)
[Subheadline] — How it works + the specific outcome (one sentence)
[CTA]         — Primary action: verb + what they get
[Trust line]  — Social proof signal: number, logos, or "no credit card required"
[Hero visual] — Product screenshot, demo video, or illustration (right side or below)
```

**Headline formula — pick one:**

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| "[Product] for [audience]" | "Analytics for indie developers" |
| "[Verb] your [outcome] in [time]" | "Ship your UI in minutes, not days" |
| "The [category] that [benefit]" | "The design system that replaces your design system" |
| "[Number] [unit] without [pain]" | "200 components. No setup required." |

**What to avoid:**
- Vague claims: "The future of X", "Reimagining Y", "The smart way to Z"
- Feature-first headlines: "AI-powered. Cloud-native. Enterprise-ready."
- Generic verbs: "Empowering", "Enabling", "Transforming"

**5-second test:** Cover the logo. Read only the headline and subheadline. Can a stranger name:
1. What the product does?
2. Who it's for?
3. What to do next?

If any answer is no → rewrite.

## Social Proof Patterns

### Above the fold (immediately after CTA)
Use numbers — specific beats vague every time:

```
Good: "Trusted by 12,000+ developers"
Bad:  "Trusted by developers worldwide"

Good: "4.9 stars · 2,400 reviews on Product Hunt"
Bad:  "Highly rated"

Good: "Used by teams at [Logo] [Logo] [Logo] [Logo] [Logo]"
Bad:  "Trusted by leading companies"
```

**Logo row rules:**
- 5–7 logos maximum
- Grayscale on light bg, white on dark bg (less distraction, more professional)
- Logos of recognizable names > logos of your biggest clients
- Add the count: "and 500+ more companies"

### Testimonials section (after features)

**Full testimonial structure:**
```
"[Specific outcome achieved, with numbers if possible.]"
— [Full name], [Title] at [Company]
[Photo] [Optional: company logo]
```

**Bad testimonial:**
> "This product is amazing. I love it."
> — John S.

**Good testimonial:**
> "We cut our design-to-code time from 2 weeks to 3 days. Our team ships twice as fast now."
> — Sarah Chen, Head of Engineering at Stripe

**Rules:**
- Full name, not initials
- Title + company = credibility signal
- Photo = 3× more credible than no photo
- Specific outcome > vague praise
- Multiple testimonials: vary by role (designer, engineer, founder), not just company

## CTA Patterns

**Primary CTA — the one action everything leads to:**

| Goal | CTA |
|------|-----|
| Free trial | Start Free Trial |
| Freemium | Get Started Free |
| Paid | Get Instant Access |
| Demo | Book a Demo |
| Download | Download for Free |
| Waitlist | Join the Waitlist |
| Contact | Talk to Sales |

**Rules:**
- One primary CTA per viewport. Secondary CTAs must be visually subordinate (ghost button or text link).
- Never two primary CTAs on the same screen — they cancel each other.
- CTA button: high contrast, large enough to see from a distance (squint test).
- Below the primary CTA: add a trust line ("No credit card required", "Free forever", "Cancel anytime").
- Final CTA section: repeat the same offer as the hero — don't introduce a new ask at the bottom.

**CTA placement:**
1. Above the fold — primary
2. After each major section — secondary (text link or ghost)
3. After testimonials — primary (second peak of trust)
4. Final section before footer — primary (for visitors who read everything)

## Problem → Solution Formula

This section creates emotional resonance. The visitor must recognize themselves in the problem.

**Problem section:**
```
[Headline]  — "Sound familiar?" or state the pain directly
[Pain 1]    — A specific frustration your audience feels
[Pain 2]    — Another frustration
[Pain 3]    — A third frustration
```

**Solution section:**
```
[Headline]  — "There's a better way" or name your approach
[Body]      — How your product solves the problem differently
[Visual]    — Before/after, diagram, or product screenshot
```

**Example:**
```
Problem:
  "Building UI takes forever."
  □ Spending weeks building components from scratch
  □ Inconsistent design across teams
  □ Every new project starts from zero

Solution:
  "Ship a complete, polished UI in one afternoon."
  Copy, paste, customize. 200+ components ready for production.
```

## Features Section Formula

Never list features — list outcomes. Every feature is a claim. Every outcome is a proof.

| Wrong (feature) | Right (outcome) |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Dark mode support | Looks perfect in dark mode — zero config |
| Customizable themes | Change your brand color in one line |
| TypeScript support | Your editor catches design errors before they ship |
| Regular updates | 5+ new components every month |
| 200+ components | Stop building what already exists |

**Feature card structure:**
```
[Icon or visual]
[Outcome headline] — "What you get"
[One sentence]     — How it achieves that outcome
```

**Features to lead with:**
1. Speed / time saved
2. Ease / reduced complexity
3. Quality / results
4. Cost / value

## Pricing Section Formula

**Always show pricing.** Hidden pricing = high exit rate. "Contact us for pricing" is a conversion killer for self-serve products.

**Pricing structure:**
```
[Headline]     — "Simple pricing, no surprises"
[3 tiers max]  — Starter / Pro / Enterprise (3 = anchoring effect)
[Highlighted]  — Most popular plan (visual emphasis)
[CTA per tier] — Each plan has its own CTA
[Trust line]   — "All plans include 30-day money-back guarantee"
```

**Tier naming:**
| Bad | Good |
|-----|------|
| Basic / Pro / Enterprise | Free / Pro / Team |
| Bronze / Silver / Gold | Starter / Growth / Scale |
| Lite / Standard / Premium | Individual / Studio / Company |

**Pricing psychology:**
- Lead with the middle tier (decoy effect — most people choose middle)
- Annual pricing first (higher perceived value)
- Show monthly equivalent: "$19/mo billed annually"
- "Most popular" badge on middle tier
- Feature comparison table: show what you get, not what you lose

## FAQ Section Formula

FAQs handle objections. Only include objections that actually prevent purchase.

**Real objections (include these):**
- "How long does it take to set up?"
- "Do I need a credit card to sign up?"
- "What happens when my trial ends?"
- "Can I cancel anytime?"
- "Is my data secure?"
- "Do you offer refunds?"

**Not real FAQs (delete these):**
- "What is [product name]?" — if you need this, your hero failed
- "Who is [product] for?" — same problem
- "How does [product] work?" — that's the features section

**Rule:** Each FAQ answer must end with a trust signal or a next step. Never end on an ambiguous note.

## Trust Signals Hierarchy

Add these in order of credibility:

1. **Named testimonials with photos** — highest trust
2. **Logo wall** — recognizable brands create instant credibility
3. **Review scores** — "4.9 ★ on Product Hunt" with link
4. **Usage numbers** — "10,000+ developers", "2M components shipped"
5. **Security/compliance** — "SOC 2", "GDPR compliant", "256-bit SSL"
6. **Guarantee** — "30-day money-back", "Cancel anytime"
7. **Press mentions** — "As seen in [Publication]"
8. **Award badges** — "Product of the Day on Product Hunt"

**Placement:**
- Numbers/logos: above the fold (after CTA)
- Reviews: after testimonials section
- Security/compliance: near pricing or CTA
- Guarantee: on the CTA button or below it

## Navigation Rules for Landing Pages

**Dedicated landing page (paid traffic, specific campaign):**
- Remove nav entirely. Only logo + primary CTA.
- No links to blog, docs, pricing, or other pages.
- Every exit = lost conversion.

**Product homepage (organic traffic, brand awareness):**
- Minimal nav: Logo | Product | Pricing | Docs | [Sign Up]
- 4–5 links maximum.
- Primary CTA in nav (not "Contact" or "Login" — those are secondary).
- Sticky nav on scroll: shows primary CTA even when hero CTA is scrolled past.

## Performance Targets

| Metric | Good | Needs Work | Critical |
|--------|------|-----------|----------|
| LCP (Largest Contentful Paint) | < 2.5s | 2.5–4s | > 4s |
| FID / INP (Interactivity) | < 100ms | 100–300ms | > 300ms |
| CLS (Layout Shift) | < 0.1 | 0.1–0.25 | > 0.25 |
| Time to Interactive | < 3.8s | 3.8–7.3s | > 7.3s |
| Total page weight | < 1MB | 1–3MB | > 3MB |

**Each 1-second delay in load time costs ~7% in conversions.**

**Common landing page performance killers:**
- Unoptimized hero image (use Next.js `<Image priority>` or `fetchpriority="high"`)
- Lazy-loaded hero image (`loading="lazy"` is wrong above the fold — use `eager`)
- Google Fonts blocking render (use `display=swap`, preconnect, preload)
- Large JS bundle (split, defer, lazy-import everything below the fold)
- No image compression (use WebP/AVIF, not PNG/JPEG)
- Render-blocking CSS (inline critical CSS, defer the rest)

**Quick wins:**
```tsx
// Hero image — priority, never lazy
<Image src="/hero.webp" alt="..." width={800} height={600} priority />

// Font preconnect (in <head>)
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com" />
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossOrigin="anonymous" />

// Below-fold sections — lazy load
const Testimonials = dynamic(() => import('./Testimonials'), { ssr: false });
const Pricing      = dynamic(() => import('./Pricing'),      { ssr: false });
```

## Social Meta Tags (OG + Twitter)

Landing pages live and die by social sharing. Bad OG tags mean bad click-through before conversion even starts.

**Required tags:**
```html
<meta property="og:title"       content="Ship UI in minutes, not days" />
<meta property="og:description" content="200+ production-ready components. Copy, paste, done." />
<meta property="og:image"       content="https://yoursite.com/og-image.png" />
<meta property="og:type"        content="website" />
<meta property="og:url"         content="https://yoursite.com" />
<meta name="twitter:card"       content="summary_large_image" />
<meta name="twitter:title"      content="Ship UI in minutes, not days" />
<meta name="twitter:description"content="200+ production-ready components. Copy, paste, done." />
<meta name="twitter:image"      content="https://yoursite.com/og-image.png" />
<meta name="description"        content="200+ production-ready components. Copy, paste, done." />
```

**OG title:** same as or tighter version of the hero headline. ≤60 characters (longer gets truncated).
**OG description:** one sentence value prop. ≤155 characters.
**OG image:** 1200×630px. Shows product UI, not a logo on a plain background.

**Test before shipping:** paste the URL into [opengraph.xyz](https://opengraph.xyz) or Twitter card validator.

## Mobile Landing Page Rules

Most landing page traffic is mobile. Test at 375px (iPhone SE) — the tightest common viewport.

**Mobile-specific issues:**
- Hero text too large → wraps awkwardly, pushes CTA below fold
- CTA button too narrow → hard to tap (minimum 44px height, full-width is better)
- Logo row → too many logos, too small at mobile width (show 3–4 max at mobile, or stack them)
- Two-column feature grid → collapses poorly (use single column on mobile)
- Pricing table → horizontal scroll is a conversion killer (use card stack on mobile)

**Mobile hero rule:** CTA must be visible at 375×812px without scrolling. Test this every time.
